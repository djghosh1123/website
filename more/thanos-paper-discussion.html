THANOS: Predicting Elections with Twitter and Polls

Can social-media activity add useful information to traditional polling? This paper explores that question by combining opinion polls with Twitter hashtag activity and network structure.

The core idea: polls are informative but slower; social media is fast but noisy.
Why combine the two?

Polling and Twitter see different parts of the campaign

Opinion polls

Designed to measure voter sentiment more systematically, but they arrive as periodic snapshots rather than a continuous stream.

+

Twitter activity

Available in real time and rich in campaign dynamics, but noisy, selective, and not representative of the full electorate.

The paper combines the depth of polling with the speed of social-media signals.
Two models

THOS and THANOS

Model 1

THOS

Twitter Hashtag-based Opinion Survey. THOS uses the frequency of campaign-related hashtags over time together with polling information.

In the paper, THOS is used as the simpler model and was most effective in examples with a clearer separation between the two sides.

Model 2

THANOS

Twitter Hashtag and Network-based Opinion Survey. THANOS adds network structure to the hashtag-and-poll framework.

The network-enhanced model was developed for closer races, where hashtag counts alone may not capture enough of the campaign structure.

The paper constructs directed retweet networks and derives features from influential users. In particular, it uses harmonic centrality and retweet-based quantities for leading influencers.

The motivation is simple: two campaigns can generate similar tweet counts while having very different network structure. A message repeatedly amplified by central users may represent a different campaign dynamic from the same number of isolated posts.

Tested on real campaigns

What the paper reported

67.3% THOS prediction for “Yes” in Ireland’s 2018 36th Amendment referendum; the reported actual vote was 66.4%.
11 of 12 THANOS correctly identified the winning candidate in 11 of the 12 closest 2018 U.S. Senate races examined in the paper.
Florida: undecided For the closest race in the set, decided by a reported 0.12% margin, the model returned an undecided forecast.
Close-race example: the paper reports that the network-enhanced model improved on the hashtag-only model in cases where the victory margin was small, including Arizona.
Robustness

What happens when tweets disappear?

The concern

Social-media datasets are incomplete. Tweets can be deleted, accounts can disappear, and historical samples may omit users.

The sensitivity analysis

The paper randomly removed up to 30% of tweets and reported that THANOS forecasts changed little. The authors attribute this stability to the emphasis on common hashtags, retweet structure, and highly influential nodes.

Big picture

Social media as a supplement, not a replacement for polls

The paper’s conclusion is not that Twitter should replace polling. Rather, it presents a hybrid forecasting framework in which traditional polls provide a structured measure of public opinion while social-media activity contributes fast-moving information about campaign attention and network dynamics.

That framing is important because Twitter users are not a representative sample of voters. The contribution is the attempt to use the platform’s dynamics as an additional predictive signal rather than treating raw tweet volume as a direct proxy for the electorate.
