  1. † Ibenye, C., † Jimenez, A.-V., † Jaeger, S. & Ghosh, D. When Machines Lie Differently: Detecting AI vs Human Fake News. Accepted at ICMLA (2026).

    Summary. This study asks whether fake news is equally detectable when it is written by humans versus generated by AI. Using the same feature representation and modeling pipeline for both tasks, the paper compares real news with human-written fake news and with AI-generated fake news using lexical-diversity, readability, and emotion features together with logistic regression, random forests, support vector machines, gradient boosting, neural networks, and an ensemble model.

    Across all models, AI-generated fake news was substantially easier to distinguish from real news: most models achieved AUCs above 0.99 for the AI-generated task, compared with roughly 0.89–0.96 for human-written fake news. Feature analyses suggest that AI-generated misinformation currently shows more uniform patterns in readability and emotional expression, whereas human-written fake news more closely resembles legitimate journalism.

    Undergraduate research project: This work was developed with undergraduate researchers Calvin Ibenye, Aya-Vera Jimenez, and Samuel Jaeger through Kennesaw State University’s First-Year Scholars Program.
